How to use Gold Trader

Gold Trader tells you when it's a good time to sell your gold, using live UK prices. It answers one question: sell now, or hold? When the time comes, it alerts your phone.

The short version
  1. Set up the phone alerts once (Alerts tab).
  2. When an alert arrives, or you're curious, open the app and read Should I sell? on the Advice page.
  3. Follow what it says. Sell in stages, never everything at once.

1. Put it on your phone

  1. iPhone: open the app in Safari, tap the Share button (square with an arrow), then Add to Home Screen.
  2. Android: open the app in Chrome, tap ⋮ at the top right, then Add to Home screen.

It then opens like a normal app, with the Dorset Jewellers icon.

2. Turn on the alerts

  1. Install the free ntfy app from the App Store or Google Play.
  2. Open it, tap +, and type your private topic name exactly as it was given to you.
  3. Tap Subscribe. You'll get a "Gold Trader alerts are on" message.

The app checks gold every hour, day and night, even when your phone is off. You'll get a message when:

🟢 Gold getting close to the sell zoneGet ready
🟡 Sell zoneSell about a quarter
🟠 Strong sell zoneSell more
✅ Sell zone overHold the rest
📅 Every Friday eveningWeekly summary

Sell alerts are rare, around once every year or two, because they only fire when gold is genuinely expensive. Silence means hold.

Why sell in stages? Gold often climbs further after first entering the sell zone. In October 2025 it entered at £93/g, ran up to £125/g in January, then dropped back. Selling a part at each stage means you never sell everything too early, and you never hold everything through a fall.

Keep a little back after the strong zone. Gold has risen a lot over the long run, and the next rise will come round.

6. How the advice works

The Advice page has a "How this decision was made" section that shows every step with today's real numbers: the price, the 40-week average, the stretch, the 4-week change, which rule matched, and the 22-year evidence behind it. There's also a decision history for the last 6 months. If anyone asks "why did it say that?", that's where to look.

The app looks at one thing: how far gold has stretched above its 40-week average, and whether it has started to turn down. That was the only measure that worked reliably when tested on 22 years of UK gold prices (2004 to today), and it held up in both 2005–2015 and 2016–2026. Popular rules like "RSI over 70" or "near its high" didn't hold up for gold, so they're not used.

The sell zone prices move slowly as the 40-week average changes, so check them now and then rather than writing them down once.
