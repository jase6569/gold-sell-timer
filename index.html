Get sell alerts on your phone

The app checks gold every hour, even when your phone is off. It sends you a notification when:

  1. gold moves into the sell zone (sell a portion), or the strong sell zone
  2. gold is getting close to the sell zone, so you can get ready
  3. the sell zone ends
  4. gold starts or stops a steep slide, which changes how much stock to hold if you buy in

You also get a short summary every Friday evening after the market closes.

How to use Gold Trader

Gold Trader is built for a gold dealer. You buy gold in every day and your profit is the gap between what you pay and what the refiner pays. Every gram you hold is a bet on the price, so the app answers one question: how much stock should I keep, and how much should I send to the refiner this week? When the answer changes, it alerts your phone.

The short version
  1. Set up the phone alerts once (Alerts tab).
  2. On the My gold tab, set up Buying in once (how much you buy in a month), and add the gold you're holding (section 5).
  3. Open the app, or wait for an alert, and read Your stock: what to do this week at the top of the Advice page. It gives the call and the grams to send to the refiner, oldest gold first.
  4. The Market check under it shows whether gold is cheap or expensive right now, and why.

Why sell in stages? Gold often climbs further after first entering the sell zone. In October 2025 it entered at £93/g, ran up to £125/g in January, then dropped back. Selling a part at each stage means you never sell everything too early, and you never hold everything through a fall.

Keep a little back after the strong zone. Gold has risen a lot over the long run, and the next rise will come round.

5. If you buy gold in all the time

Most of your profit comes from the gap between what you pay customers and what the refiner pays you. You only lock that in when you sell. Every week the gold sits in the safe, you're betting on the price. Buying in on the My gold tab keeps that bet small and tells you exactly what to send off.

Set it up once
  1. Go to My gold and add the gold you hold now: weight, carat and a short note. New items get today's date, so the app knows which is oldest.
  2. In Buying in, enter roughly how many grams you buy in a month (all carats together).
  3. Enter the most stock you're happy to hold, in months of buying. Start with 2.
  4. In Quick check, set Buyer pays to your refiner's real percentage, so the £ figures are right.
Each week
  1. Add what you bought in as you go, or once a week.
  2. On Friday (the summary alert reminds you), open My gold. It shows how many grams of each carat to send to the refiner, oldest gold first, and roughly what you'll be paid.
  3. If it shows a timing tip, a big US or UK announcement is due. Send the batch after the result is out.
  4. Once it's gone, tap I've sent this batch. The grams come off your list, so next week's figures are right.

Good habits
  1. Buy in at today's price, not last week's, especially when gold is falling.
  2. Sort by carat before selling. Mixed lots tend to get paid at the lowest carat.
  3. Get quotes from two or three refiners now and then. A few percent better payout is worth more than most timing calls.
  4. Keep one stock list. Each phone keeps its own list, so if two people share the same stock, only one of you should keep it.

Selling into a sudden drop isn't part of the plan. In past UK prices, new 3-month lows were more often followed by a bounce than by a further fall, so the app doesn't tell you to panic-sell on a dip. It just stops you piling up stock in a long slide.

7. How the advice works

The Advice page has a "How this decision was made" section that shows every step with today's real numbers: the price, the 40-week average, the stretch, the 4-week change, which rule matched, and the 22-year evidence behind it. There's also a decision history for the last 6 months. If anyone asks "why did it say that?", that's where to look.

The app looks at one thing: how far gold has stretched above its 40-week average, and whether it has started to turn down. That was the only measure that worked reliably when tested on 22 years of UK gold prices (2004 to today), and it held up in both 2005–2015 and 2016–2026. Popular rules like "RSI over 70" or "near its high" didn't hold up for gold, so they're not used.

The sell zone prices move slowly as the 40-week average changes, so check them now and then rather than writing them down once.

Questions

The price looks a little different from my buyer's. The app shows the market (spot) price. Buyers pay a percentage of it and some quote differently, so use the Value page with your buyer's percentage.

It says "Live feed unavailable". The app is showing the last saved price and will keep retrying every minute. The advice still works.

I haven't had an alert for ages. That's normal. Alerts only come when gold nears or enters the sell zone, plus the Friday summary. If even the Friday summary stops, check you're still subscribed in ntfy.

Buying in says send gold, but the main call says hold. Which is right? Both. The main call is about a private holding you can keep for as long as you like. Buying in is for a shop that's always buying more. There, the safe way is to keep stock to a limit and send the rest, even when gold is cheap.

Is it always right? No. It's based on what has happened before, and big world events can change things fast. It's a strong guide, not a guarantee, and it isn't regulated financial advice. The decision is always yours.
